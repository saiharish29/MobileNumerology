Feng Shui · Eight Mansions · Personal directions

What is a Kua number?

A single number from 1 to 9 (excluding 5) derived from your birth year and gender. In the Eight Mansions (Ba Zhai) school of feng shui, your Kua splits the compass into four auspicious and four inauspicious directions for you specifically.

Why it matters: facing one of your good directions while sleeping, working or sitting down to meals is said to draw on supportive energy; facing a bad direction does the opposite. Most useful when orienting your bed-head, desk and front door.

East vs. West group: every Kua belongs to one of two groups. East group (1, 3, 4, 9) shares the same four good directions; West group (2, 6, 7, 8) shares a different four. Partners often coordinate their home around the group both belong to.

The year is what matters most. If you were born in Jan or early Feb, see Li Chun option below.
Formula & Li Chun explained

Step 1: add the last two digits of your birth year. Reduce to a single digit.

Step 2: apply by gender and era:

  • Male, year < 2000: Kua = 10 − single digit
  • Male, year ≥ 2000: Kua = 9 − single digit
  • Female, year < 2000: Kua = single digit + 5 (reduce)
  • Female, year ≥ 2000: Kua = single digit + 6 (reduce)

Step 3 (Kua 5): if the result is 5, men use 2 and women use 8 instead. (Kua 5 has no place in the Eight Mansions system.)

Li Chun adjustment: the Chinese solar year begins around February 4 (the festival of Li Chun, "Beginning of Spring"). Births before this date are traditionally counted as belonging to the previous year for Kua purposes. Skipping this can flip you into the other group.
